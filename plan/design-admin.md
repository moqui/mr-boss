IronStone Admin serves system administrators and people delegated a particular administrative responsibility. It brings together users, groups and access; organizations, settings and the shared human/agent Constitution; integrations, mail and protocol configuration; jobs and operational health; imports, exports, audit and privacy. Business applications continue to own their policies and transactions. Admin makes their configuration, readiness and recovery discoverable without becoming another editor for the same records. The first release is small: users, groups, sessions, invitations, organizations and the Constitution, the settings index, health, and the framework System screens mounted for `ADMIN` (§17.3).

## 1. Shell

### 1.1 Root, authority and presentation

Root `screen/Admin.xml`, route `Admin`, keeps icon `sym_o_admin_panel_settings`. `BOSS_ADMIN` is the existing artifact group, and `BOSS_ADMIN_ADMIN` is an existing setup user group. `BOSS_ADMIN` is also the root user group (impl-admin P1, Commerce's pattern): every ordinary Admin user is in it and a capability group, and an area only some groups see denies it and allows theirs. The root's comment saying its grants are commented out is stale: setup has framework ADMIN and app-admin authorizations with `MANTLE_ACTIVE_ORG` and `MANTLE_PRIVATE_CONTACT`. The root and Home placeholder are not the workflows specified here.

The root continues `mantle.party.PartyServices.setup#UserOrganizationInfo`. Internal organizations are multiple parties in one instance, not separate installations. Ordinary delegated groups take the existing user-organization and private-contact filters; the administrator setup group keeps its established filters. Every read and action independently checks its permission and target scope. An account belongs to one identity even when that identity works for several organizations. Authority to manage one organization's memberships does not authorize disabling that person's entire account, inspecting another organization's membership or granting instance-wide access.

Groups are assignment bundles. Services use `ec.user.hasPermission` and domain scope, never literal BOSS group checks, calling-screen names or a supplied `appRoot`. A write permission is not a view permission. The framework seeds the `ADMIN_*` permissions, each granted to `ADMIN`, and BossSetupData gives the proposed groups below their listed permissions (impl-admin §4); an installation may assign the permissions through its own groups. A root grant only opens navigation. Framework privileged-group and sealed-permission enforcement remains in force even for an app administrator.

These groups and permissions are rows of crosscut §1.4's capability table, beside every other app's. `ADMIN_CONSTITUTION_GLOBAL`, built with the Constitution and sealed by the framework, is a separate sealed permission for instance-wide Constitution save/reset, granted to instance administrators; neither `AgentAdmin` nor an organization configuration delegation implies it. Organization save/reset uses `ADMIN_SETTINGS_MANAGE` with independently checked organization scope. Constitution reading is available to signed-in users for the global document and to members or authorized administrators for their organizations, without general Admin or AgentAdmin access (§7).

`ADMIN_LOGIN_AS`, `ADMIN_PASSWORD`, `SQL_RUNNER_WEB`, `GROOVY_SHELL_WEB`, `SSH_SHELL`, `LOAD_RUNNER`, `SearchRemote`, `DashboardRemote`, `REST_SCHEMA`, `LlmGatewayUse`, `COMPONENT_DEPLOY`, `DATABASE_ADMIN`, `DATABASE_EXTERNAL`, `ADMIN_CONSTITUTION_GLOBAL` and Admin's `ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL`, `ADMIN_ACCESS_MANAGE` and `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD` are the framework's sealed permissions, and `ADMIN`, `ADMIN_ADV` and `SYSMSG_RECEIVE` its privileged groups (`user-facade` `authz` in `MoquiDefaultConf.xml`; [Attack-Surface](../../../../../docs/security/Attack-Surface.md)). mr-boss adds `BOSS_ADMIN_ACCESS`, `BOSS_ADMIN_BACKUP` and `BOSS_ADMIN_ADMIN` to the privileged groups with `authz` `privileged-group` elements in its `MoquiConf.xml`, which the conf merge adds to the framework's list rather than replacing it (impl-admin P1). The framework EECAs `UserGroupPermissionSealed` and `UserGroupMemberPrivileged` call `crates/moqui-core/src/service/security_checks.rs`: only a holder of a sealed permission may write a `UserGroupPermission` row for it, and only a member of a privileged group may change its membership; `UserGroupMemberSealed` (impl-admin F-P3-3, review R1) lets only a holder of each sealed permission a group grants give a membership in it, so a sealed grant is not passed on through a group that is not privileged.

**Sealed Admin grants** (proposed, ADM-ACCESS). The framework seeds `ADMIN_BACKUP_DOWNLOAD`, `ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL` and `ADMIN_ACCESS_MANAGE` and adds them to `MoquiDefaultConf.xml`'s `sealed-permissions`, beside its `ADMIN_CONSTITUTION_GLOBAL`, so a framework-only instance keeps them sealed (decided Nov 7, 2026; [impl-admin](impl-admin.md) §4). mr-boss `MoquiConf.xml` adds `BOSS_ADMIN_ACCESS`, `BOSS_ADMIN_BACKUP` and `BOSS_ADMIN_ADMIN` to the privileged groups.

A component's `authz` attribute replaces the framework's value (`merge_user_facade` merges the element), so a component's lists would have to repeat the framework IDs. Instead the framework gains child elements of `authz`, `sealed-permission` and `privileged-group`, that each component adds to the lists without replacing the framework's defaults, and mr-boss names only its own groups that way (decided Nov 7, 2026; impl-admin §4). The framework's sealed list now also holds `COMPONENT_DEPLOY`, `DATABASE_ADMIN`, `DATABASE_EXTERNAL` and `ADMIN_CONSTITUTION_GLOBAL` (impl-admin F5). Bounded delegation is then enforced below Admin: an access administrator grants only registered bundles, can never grant a sealed permission it does not hold, and cannot add anyone to a privileged group it is not in. The same rules hold for direct service calls, imports and SSO role mappings. Admin also protects the last usable administration path when a login policy or membership changes: framework `org.moqui.admin.IdentityServices.check#AdministrationPath` (built in impl-admin P3), called by every grant, membership and group-flag change, by the factor and lifecycle changes, and by a change that disables a sign-in provider (impl-admin P10: an administrator with no password is a path only while a provider is enabled). A usable path is a date-valid member of `ADMIN` (the framework names no app's administrator group, and after impl-admin F34 no BOSS group holds `ALWAYS` on the Admin root) who is not permanently disabled or terminated, can authenticate, holds a date-valid factor where any of its groups requires one and is admitted by its own and its groups' `ipAllowed` from the acting administrator's address, now and at each later date the change names (impl-admin F41, F-P3-1). A change that would leave none where there was one is refused with a reason.

XML widgets are the default. Every designed screen renders in `qvt` and `tui`; lists have paging, saved filters, useful phone columns and names instead of unlabelled IDs. Detail screens use the shared record header, one primary action by state, a More menu and named confirmations. Destructive, bulk, credential and external-send actions show the target/count and consequences. Phones support triage, identity disablement with confirmation, invitation status and incident links; wide matrices have an equivalent filtered list. Secrets, private report bodies and sensitive HR details never enter shell context or a hidden client column.

### 1.4 Screens that are the framework's

Admin is the suite's administration app, and it is not the only place an instance is administered. What has an organization or a business owner in it is Admin's: staff identities in their organizations, invitations and lifecycle, organizations and their Constitution documents, reference data, shared devices, the access log, Home, and the registrations of the BOSS apps. The administration of framework records through `org.moqui.admin` has no business model in it, so those screens are the framework's System app (`runtime/components/base/tools/screen/System/`), which an instance without BOSS has too, and Admin shows them under its own root. The rules and the record of the move are mr-moqui's: [plans/archive/Admin-Screens-Down.md](../../../../../plans/archive/Admin-Screens-Down.md) (Track A, phases A0 to A6) and the tools component's [README](../../../../../runtime/components/base/tools/README.md#mounting-the-administration-screens) ("Mounting the administration screens"). Tools is neither Admin's nor mounted: Home links it for a user whose own grant opens it.

A System screen is shown in one of three ways:
- **By location.** The area's `subscreens-item` names the System file and Admin has no file of its own. Only where no BOSS grant names the screen, it has no transition of the instance administrator's, and Admin adds nothing.
- **A wrapper** at Admin's own location: the System screen included, with one `transition-include` for each transition Admin offers. A transition that is not taken in does not exist on Admin's route.
- **Admin's own screen** around the System screen's named sections and forms, with its own actions and transitions, where Admin adds an organization scope.

**The mount.** The root sets `adminMount` in its `always-actions` (the `mount` of `get#SharedContext`): where a System screen's link to a record goes under Admin, by the registrations' own keys (a recovery descriptor's or a transfer target's `ownerRoute`, an audit projection's `linkKey`, a readiness provider's `fixRoute`, a settings entry's `destinationRoute`). `mountRoutes` and `settingsRoutes` in `service/ironstone/admin/Admin.js` are the one table of them: Admin's own records (a user, the invitations, a lifecycle operation, an organization), the import wizard, and the other apps' pages as plain URLs. A framework record whose page is the same relative path under both roots needs no entry. The map is data and gives no right.

**Grants.** No BOSS group is granted a System screen's location, which would open it under `/apps/system` too. The area grants (`BOSS_ADM_<Area>`) and the screen grants name Admin's own locations, which is why a screen a grant names is a wrapper. ## 4. Users and lifecycle

### 4.1 Identity contract

The canonical identity is UserAccount linked to Party. Admin handles staff account administration; Commons handles the user's own preferences, factors and credentials; People owns employment and confidential personnel information. UserDetail links Employee360 for its authorized audience; Admin does not mount Employee360. Employee360 links here with `partyId`/`userId` and returns to its authorized host. The Contact record links the same account forms for an authorized identity operator. These links convey no authority and expose no HR details by default.

**Service placement.** Identity work scoped to an organization uses Mantle's PartyRelationship and PartyRole, so it lives in proposed Mantle `mantle.party.IdentityServices`: users in scope, invitations, organization membership and lifecycle operations. The framework keeps only generic guards, in proposed `org.moqui.admin.IdentityServices`:
- `check#AdministrationPath`;
- `revoke#AccountAccess`, which revokes sessions, login keys, app passwords, SSH keys and SIP phones and pauses the agent triggers that run as the user (built in impl-admin P4, with `get#AccountCredentials`, metadata only);
- `find#Sessions` and `revoke#Sessions` (built in P4).

These framework services do not depend on Mantle. Group and grant governance stays in framework `org.moqui.admin.AccessServices`, since UserGroup is a framework record.

**Invitations.** Staff invitations are proposed Mantle `mantle.party.UserInvitation` records, separate from PASS PublicSiteInvitation. They bind a verified destination, party, organization membership and approved grant proposal to a single-use hashed token with expiry. Sending, resending and revoking are audited; a resend invalidates the previous token. The invitation email is an EmailTemplate of the existing EmailType `EMT_ACCOUNT_INVITE`; its link opens AcceptInvitation (`/i/<token>`). Acceptance requires proof of the invited address (the mailed token) and either an authenticated matching account or controlled account creation. Existing identities are matched explicitly; a similar name is never sufficient. Acceptance rechecks inviter delegation, target scope and current group grants. Invitation metadata cannot assign ADMIN, sealed rights or a new organization through a public redemption request. Tokens never appear in lists, logs, shell context or exported error files. When the instance cannot send mail (no SMTP host), InviteStaff's or a resend's result shows the acceptance link once, as plain text with a copy control rather than a link, for the inviter to pass on; showing it is audited, and the token is never stored in plain text or shown again (decided Nov 7, 2026; impl-admin §4). As built (impl-admin P5): an invitation never carries a privileged group or a group granting a sealed permission, whoever sends it; acceptance rechecks from the records what the inviter could give now (account usable, `ADMIN_IDENTITY_MANAGE`, the organization theirs, each bundle registered and delegable by them), since the framework checks only as the current user, and writes the organization relationship and the memberships itself with authorization on, so the sealed and privileged EECAs still decide each; the redemption request names neither organization nor access (impl-admin F-P5-2). An invitation that would bring an existing identity into the organization (the address or chosen person belongs to a site identity, an account of no internal organization, or to staff of an organization the inviter does not administer) needs `ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL`, as relating that person directly does: checked as the inviter when it is sent or resent, and for the inviter from the records against the redeeming account at acceptance (impl-admin review Nov 7 (P5), F-P5-8).

Account-wide login/email changes, factor resets and credential revocation on an existing identity require identity-global authority (`ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL`, sealed) as well as the action's own permission. A delegated organization operator manages that organization's relationship and approved grants only. These administrative actions are conveniences for those holding the screens and permissions, not the permission boundary; like the System app's they may require their own permissions (decided Nov 7, 2026). As built (impl-admin P4 and its follow-up): who sees which user records follows the existing authorization patterns, the screens' authorization and then the active and available organization entity filters. An account has no organization of its own, so it is found through Mantle's view `mantle.party.UserAccountOrganization`, which joins it to the internal organizations its party works for (a current agent, employee or member relationship) with the organization aliased `organizationPartyId`, and the filter sets `MANTLE_USER_ORG` and `MANTLE_ACTIVE_ORG` carry rows for it (and for `mantle.party.InternalOrganization`, the organizations administered). An `ADMIN` member, and the administrator `BOSS_ADMIN_ADMIN`, whose grants carry `MANTLE_ACTIVE_ORG`, sees every staff identity with no organization active and only that organization's with one active; the identity operator's grant carries `MANTLE_USER_ORG`, its own organizations (the active one when set). A call under no screen grant runs under Mantle's `ALL_USERS` grant of the identity services, which carries `MANTLE_USER_ORG`, or for `ADMIN` under its own grant of them, which carries `MANTLE_ACTIVE_ORG`; one whose authorization names neither filter set has nothing in scope but for `ADMIN`. An identity is readable when one of its organizations is in scope, and `ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL` is asked for an account-wide change (the account's fields, a factor or lock reset, revoking its sessions or credentials) only of an identity that also works for an organization outside that scope; an identity wholly within it is the operator's. A record with no organization filter of its own is found through such a view the same way: an account's Chatter notes follow the account's view, and a security group's (instance-wide, no organization) are an access viewer's (`ADMIN_ACCESS_VIEW`). A password reset request (the change-only reset password `reset#Password` mails) changes nothing until its owner acts, so it is sent for any identity in scope. An account-wide change, a session or credential revocation and Login as are refused for an identity in a privileged group the operator is not in or holding a sealed permission the operator lacks, and bringing an identity of no organization (a site identity) into one, or relating another organization's staff to one's own (which would make them readable), needs `ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL`, `ADMIN`'s by default (impl-admin review R1, R2, F78). The account's owner may still perform the separately guarded Commons self-service actions. This prevents one employer's administrator from taking over an identity shared with another organization.

**Provisioning from People.** People commits a hire or employment change first. A proposed mr-boss SECA (`when="tx-commit"`) on proposed `mantle.humanres.PeopleServices.hire#Employee` and `apply#EmploymentChange` then calls proposed `ironstone.admin.LifecycleServices.request#Provisioning` or `request#AccessEnd`. The SECA lives in mr-boss, so Mantle does not depend on BOSS.
- **Key.** The request is keyed by partyId, organizationPartyId, employment key (the employment PartyRelationship), the approved grant proposal and the People operationKey. A retry with the same key returns the same operation, so it cannot create a second identity or invitation.
- **Steps.** The operation (proposed `mantle.party.UserLifecycleOperation`) records independent steps: account or invitation, organization membership, approved grants and notifications.
- **IT checklist task.** People's IT checklist task carries the lifecycle `operationId`, so completing the task links the account outcome.
- **Grant proposal.** A hire's provisioning proposes `BOSS_USERS` and `BOSS_PEOPLE_SELF` by default, and the account request is optional (no login merely for payroll); anything more HR chooses from the bundles HR may grant, and Admin's delegation rules decide (decided Nov 11, 2026, the user's call: impl-people §4, built in its P7).
- **As built (impl-people P7).** Mantle's `mantle.party.IdentityServices.request#Provisioning` and `request#AccessEnd` (Lifecycle.js) take an applied People change (`EmploymentChange`): a hire whose HR officer asked an account for, or a separation. Each records one `UserLifecycleOperation` of the employer keyed by the change (`people-provision-` or `people-access-end-` and its id; its `sourceReference` the change), Provisioning (`UlkProvision`; userId empty until there is an account) with the steps membership (the employment is the organization membership, F49: no second relationship), invite (`invite#User` of the person's work address with the proposal, or skipped for an existing account), grants (given at the invitation's acceptance, or `apply#GrantProposal` for an existing account) and notify (the invitation's mail), or an Organization removal from the separation's date with the employer's other relationships ended and, when the person then works for no internal organization, the sessions and memberships (else the memberships step skipped, naming the organization still worked for). The request is admitted for the change's requester, an HR editor of its employer, the one whose decision applied it, or an identity operator of it. Its steps run at once only when the caller is an identity operator of the organization (`ADMIN_IDENTITY_MANAGE`, the organization in scope), with the same checks as `start#Lifecycle`; for anyone else they wait (`OPERATOR_REQUIRED`, "Waiting for an identity operator" in LifecycleOperations and LifecycleDetail), and the operator runs each with Retry (`retry#LifecycleStep`), as themselves, so the invitation's inviter is a real operator whose delegation rules decide. The operation's own records are written with authorization off once the request is checked. BOSS's `ironstone.admin.LifecycleServices.request#Provisioning` and `request#AccessEnd` (Provisioning.js) add the proposal (`BOSS_USERS`, `BOSS_PEOPLE_SELF`, any other bundle only one the caller may give: registered, its delegable permission held, not privileged) and note a failure on the change (`PeopleServices.note#AccessRequestFailure`) instead of failing; the tx-commit rules of `service/PeopleLifecycle.secas.xml` call them after People's hire and an applied separation (`apply#`, `decide#` and `approve#EmploymentChange`, the last two because a separation is applied by its approval), and any user may call them (the `BOSS_PPL_ACCESS_REQUEST` grant, Mantle admitting only the change's own people). People's IT checklist task carries the operation (`ChecklistStep.operationId`) and shows its state and its steps' states. `create#StaffAccount` is not used: a hire is invited, never given a direct account. Review of P7 (the user's calls, Nov 11): steps left waiting notify the employer's identity operators (users holding `ADMIN_IDENTITY_MANAGE` through a group, whose organizations include the employer, not the requester or the person) on the `IdentityOperatorRequest` topic, the title naming the person and employer only, opening LifecycleDetail; `request#Provisioning` itself refuses a bundle the caller may not give by Admin's delegation rule (`GRANT_NOT_YOURS`), as BOSS's entry does; and a separation withdrawn before its date withdraws its access end (`UlopWithdrawn`: waiting steps skipped, the memberships and relationships it ended at the date continued, never a privileged group's).

Direct account creation remains for explicitly authorized service accounts and exceptional administration; it is not the default hire path.

**The BOSS users group.** Every BOSS app requires a UserGroup; `ALL_USERS` grants none (crosscut §1.4). Provisioning an internal user, by a staff invitation, a People hire or NewStaffAccount, adds `BOSS_USERS` ("IronStone BOSS users", which opens Commons, Comms and the Records drive) with the app groups of the approved grant proposal, and offboarding ends it with them; a PASS or e-commerce public account never receives it. Until Admin's Users screens are built, the paths are the tools' UserAccount screens (add the group membership there) and Mantle's `mantle.party.PartyServices.create#PartyUserAccount` with `userGroupId` `BOSS_USERS`, followed by the app groups.

**Passwords.** Admin never sets or mails a password an administrator chose. A reset uses existing `org.moqui.impl.UserServices.reset#Password`:
- it mails a random reset password through the `PASSWORD_RESET` template;
- that password can only be used to change the password, and the current password stays valid;
- it expires after `user-facade` `password` `email-expire-hours` (48 by default).

Setting another user's password needs the sealed `ADMIN_PASSWORD`.

**Offboarding.** Ending access to one organization differs from globally disabling the identity.
- **Preview.** It names grants, credentials, sessions, owned reports/alerts/jobs/agent triggers, shared mail resources and outstanding assignments. A confidential People case the user handles is not an outstanding assignment (impl-people P21); People's lifecycle participant lists each open one as "Confidential case", by name alone and not transferable (its review, the user's call of Nov 12, 2026): when the user leaves the employer, the case goes back to the employer's case intake handlers, who are told.
- **Global disable.** It blocks authentication at once and revokes sessions, login keys, app passwords and SSH access through `revoke#AccountAccess`. A future effective date is written to `UserAccount.terminateDate`, so sign-in stops at that date without a job.
- **Organization removal.** It ends that organization's relationships and grants without touching unrelated access.

Pending runs and deliveries recheck authority. Audit and history stay. Owned report schedules and alert rules are paused through the Insights participant's admin-callable proposed `org.moqui.report.ReportServices.pause#OwnedSchedules(userId)`. They stay visibly paused until a qualified new owner accepts them through proposed `transfer#DefinitionOwner` (Insights INS-06/INS-07). Admin sees safe status only. As built (impl-insights P7): the participant `insights.schedules` is the report engine's `find#OwnedSchedules`, `pause#OwnedSchedules` and `transfer#DefinitionOwner` (`REPORT_OWNER_CHANGE`, the identity operator's and the administrator's); a disable runs its pause step and leaves a transfer step for each report schedule, and a transferred schedule stays paused until its new owner resumes it, which checks its recipients against the new owner's scope. As built (impl-insights P8): alert rules have a participant of their own, `insights.alerts` (the engine's `find#OwnedAlertRules`, `pause#OwnedAlertRules` and `transfer#AlertRuleOwner`, the same permission), so a disable also pauses the leaver's alert rules and leaves a transfer step for each, and a transferred rule stays paused until its new owner activates it. Other resources move through their domain's registered participant service, never through generic Party reassignment. Disablement never authorizes deletion of personal contacts, messages, financial history or HR evidence; §12 applies.

As built (impl-admin P6): an operation is Mantle's `UserLifecycleOperation` with independent `UserLifecycleOperationStep`s, kinds disable, organization removal and restore. A disable at once revokes access first (`revoke#AccountAccess`, its own transaction, so it stays revoked whatever follows) and then disables the account for good (`disabled` with no `disabledDateTime`; the framework's `disable#UserAccount` sets a time and so is a tarpit lock that expires, impl-admin F-P6-2); with a future date it writes only `terminateDate`. An organization removal ends that organization's relationships (now or at the date); when the identity works for no other internal organization it also signs it out and ends its group memberships (the sealed and privileged EECAs deciding each), and only then do owned resources move; otherwise memberships, credentials and resources stay for the organizations it still works for. A restore enables the account and clears its end date; revoked keys, app passwords and SSH keys stay revoked, and ended memberships and paused schedules stay. Global operations are account-wide (`ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL` for an identity shared beyond the caller's scope, never an account holding a privileged group or sealed permission the caller lacks), a removal that ends memberships is too, no one changes their own account here, and every disable or membership end is checked against the administration path. An operation carries the organization it was requested for (the one removed, or for a global operation the target's organization in the requester's scope), and the organization entity filters (`MANTLE_USER_ORG`, `MANTLE_ACTIVE_ORG`) apply to it, so it stays readable in that organization after the identity has left. Participants' steps come from BOSS's registry (`ironstone.admin.LifecycleServices.find#OwnedResources`): a pause runs at once, each owned resource waits for a new owner; Mantle calls only a service carrying the framework's owned-resource contract (`org.moqui.admin.IdentityServices.define#OwnedResourceLookup`, `Transfer`, `Pause`), through the framework's `call#OwnedResourceService`, so it runs under its own authorization and never Mantle's inheritable grant; a new owner is never the administrator moving it nor an external user; and BOSS's `transfer#OwnedResource` checks the step names what its participant registered.

As built (impl-admin P10): `Admin/Security/IdentityProviderDetail`, over `ConfigurationServices.find#IdentityProviders`, `store#IdentityProvider` (at the provider's revision) and `test#IdentityProvider`. Whoever configures a provider decides who signs in as the accounts linked to it, so its parts are held apart by permission:

- **ProviderConfiguration** (`ADMIN_CONNECTION_MANAGE`): the type's own form. A secret is entered and never shown, only whether one is stored. A change of the client type, client id, issuer or discovery URL, Keycloak base URL or realm, an OAuth endpoint, the Apple team or key (SAML: the metadata location or entity id) needs the secret entered again in the same save, else the stored one is cleared; either way the provider is saved disabled and its test mark cleared, as a change of the token signature or the nonce does with the secret kept. URLs are `https` (`http` only for the server's own machine on an instance that is not production). On a production instance a provider started here reaches no internal address (`AuthFlow.allowPrivateNetwork` `N`, which the sign-in's own client enforces for every request of the provider and this service never changes): an instance administrator allows a provider on the instance's own network. The audit log keeps before and after, a secret as replaced or cleared with no value.
- **IdentityMappings**: a claim name onto the account's full name, email address, locale or time zone (`ADMIN_CONNECTION_MANAGE`); a provider whose rows carry an expression or a mapping service keeps them, read only (Tools'). The matching rule is the provider's link rule (`AuthFlow.accountLinkEnumId`, `ADMIN_ACCESS_MANAGE`): never (the default: an administrator links an existing account), or by the provider's verified email address equal to the account's. The sign-in enforces it: a provider's username alone links nothing, and an account in a privileged group or in a group granting a sealed permission is never linked by a sign-in. A link is the provider's id with the provider it is of (`UserAccount.externalAuthFlowId`, which the sign-in sets): a second provider never signs in the first one's account by asserting its id. A claim name is read as that claim and never evaluated.
- **RoleMappings** (`ADMIN_ACCESS_MANAGE`): a remote role to a registered bundle the administrator may assign (the picker offers only those; the grant proposal's rules), never a privileged group or one that grants a sealed permission, whoever asks; the same for the default group. A refused row is named with its reason, and a row that no longer holds is shown as not given. The sign-in gives and ends only the groups the role mappings name and passes over a group it may not give, so memberships an administrator gave by hand stay.
- **ProviderCheck**: Test checks that the configuration is complete, that every mapped group may still be given, and that a sign-in can start (the server reads an OpenID provider's discovery document and builds its client as a sign-in does; an OAuth provider's endpoints are checked as URLs). No account is made, nobody is signed in and no token is asked for, so the secret is proven only by the first real sign-in. With a test identity's roles it says which group each would give. A pass is marked for the configuration it ran against.
- **Enabling** (`ADMIN_ACCESS_MANAGE`) needs that pass for the configuration as it is now and role mappings that still hold, and is refused while an account this provider signs in (one it is named on, or one that names no provider yet) holds access beyond the administrator's own (a privileged group they are not in, a sealed permission they lack): an instance administrator enables it. Disabling keeps the administration path (an administrator with no password signs in only through an enabled provider).

**Since Admin-Screens-Down A3** (§1.4): the framework's System › Security › IdentityProviderDetail, mounted by location; Admin adds nothing.

A device identifies no person, but each dashboard it plays is read as someone, so a registration names a display account: a UserAccount marked `displayAccount`, with no password, email or external identity, which nothing signs in (a password, a key, an app password, an SSH key, an identity provider, a factor, a reset and Login as are refused by the framework). - The display account is the user of a device's request and of nothing else. - A device's request is refused everything outside its route group, by the framework, before the target runs: the route group is an allow-list (an artifact group that grants nothing), and what it admits is still authorized as the display account. Keys, Basic credentials and login parameters are not read on a device's request.
- A registrar may name only a display account in their identity scope (another organization's is not available; one that also shows an organization they do not administer needs `ADMIN_IDENTITY_GLOBAL`) whose every group they could assign by §1.1's delegation rules, so a device gives nothing its registrar could not give a person. The display account is checked again on every request of its devices: disabled, given a password, a privileged group or a sealed permission, its devices are refused until that is put right.

## 8. Integrations and system messages

### 8.1 Connection and recovery boundary

**Ownership (review Z4).** Admin Connections owns the gateway, remote and OAuth configuration records: their endpoints, service names and credentials. These are the native `PaymentGatewayConfig`, `ShippingGatewayConfig`, `TaxGatewayConfig`, `SystemMessageRemote`, EmailOAuthProvider and NetworkPrinter records, used directly rather than copied into a universal Connection entity. A registry gives them a common list/detail presentation and registered operations. New fields are added to the native record only when necessary.

Accounting, Warehouse and Commerce own the bindings and the selection:
- Accounting: the payment method and gateway binding (PaymentMethods), the tax gateway selection (Tax), and payment and tax business rules;
- Warehouse: carrier and shipment-method bindings, and facility printer bindings (FacilityPrinter);
- Commerce: store options. It offers only the store organization's enabled `PaymentGatewayOrganization` rows.

Provider URLs and executable service names are configuration authority, never inputs on a finance, warehouse or store form.

**Organization availability.** Admin provisions `PaymentGatewayOrganization`, the only organization-availability record that exists, and OrganizationAvailability covers payment gateways only. Shipping, tax, mail and remote connections are available to every organization the binding owner permits; a general availability record for them is later, if a deployment needs one.

**Credentials.** Reads return masked credentials, provider identity, allowed organization use and readiness. A credential replacement is write-only and audited, and an explicit test follows it. Existing encrypted connection fields remain encrypted. Deployment keys remain configuration/environment; this is not a new credential-vault implementation.

**Endpoint change (review Z1).** A change to any endpoint, host, URL, port or auth-type field of a connection clears the stored secrets that depend on it, or requires them to be entered again in the same save. Examples: a gateway's endpoint, a remote's `sendUrl`, a relay's `smtpHost`/`smtpPort`/`authTypeEnumId`, a provider's issuer. The connection is saved disabled and stays disabled until an explicit test against the new endpoint passes. The audit records before and after values, with secrets masked. Without this rule an administrator could point a stored password or token at a host of their choosing without ever reading it.

SystemMessage/job retry is an owner action, not permission to repeat a business effect. A registered recovery descriptor names original operation identity, evidence, eligible states, required permission, reconciliation action and safe retry action. Payments, carrier labels, stock postings, promotional submissions and external channel imports retain their domain rules. Unknown external outcomes must be reconciled before resubmission. Transport attempts, business acceptance and settlement are separate states. Independent items continue after a named failure; a dependent posting remains atomic.

A target that mounts Shared/DataImport and whose design names other verbs (`preview#`, `import#`) provides the five steps under those names, so its services follow Products'. **An app's own import permission** (decided Nov 11, 2026, the user's call: impl-admin §4, impl-sales §4). The People, Maintenance, Sales and Marketing targets each name an import permission of their app (`PEOPLE_IMPORT`, `MAINTENANCE_IMPORT`, `SALES_IMPORT`, `MARKETING_AUDIENCE_IMPORT`) beside the app's edit permission, and no `ADMIN_*` permission: a rows-mode target requires every permission it lists, so `ADMIN_TRANSFER_RUN` beside them would shut the app's own users out, and a wizard-mode target's permission is checked by its owner's wizard services. Admin's Imports lists such a run for a data operator who also holds the app's view permission.

**Contract.** Admin supplies a catalog, staging UI and consistent results envelope. Each registered target names:
- its owning services and the view, import and export permissions it requires;
- the supported format and version, and the permitted fields;
- the organization or record scope, and the matching and replay rules;
- nonmutating validation, the apply action, the error projection and the canonical result route.

A target wraps its domain batch; it does not copy that batch's rows into a second ledger.

The sequence is Upload → select sheet/header/options → map → validate/preview → confirm → apply → results. File size/row limits and permitted types are target-specific and shown before upload. CSV and supported spreadsheets use existing parsers, including `org.moqui.resource.SpreadsheetServices.read#Spreadsheet`. Each domain write calls its real business service, preserving permission, effective-date, accounting, stock and automation rules. `load#SpreadsheetEntity` remains an advanced generic loader, not the business executor.

Validation is nonmutating. It reports unknown fields, ambiguous identities, missing references, scope violations, conflicts and anticipated side effects. A preview records file/mapping/target version and relevant source revisions; apply refuses a stale preview and reruns validation. Never run “test” by sending email or a payment and rolling back database rows. Independent rows commit separately; dependent document lines form an explicitly declared atomic unit. Source key plus target/organization and operation identity makes retry deterministic; identical retries return the prior result and changed content under that identity is refused. Historical imports do not generate new obligations, stock or messages unless the selected domain import mode explicitly authorizes them.

Secrets, password hashes, arbitrary groups, privileged authz, executable service names and raw policy expressions are not business-import columns. People protects payroll history, Accounting protects posted meaning, Products protects catalog revisions, Warehouse protects stock posting, and Commerce channel sync retains its SystemMessage intake. Error downloads are permission-checked and redact inaccessible/secret fields. Retention removes staged files only under the registered retention policy.

Safe result envelopes carry provider/key, canonical source identity, state, reasonCode, user-safe message, revision, checked/completed time, authorized link and counts only when permitted. All operations revalidate current permission and scope, and mutating ones use expected revision plus stable operation identity where retries can repeat effects.

### 17.4 Later capabilities

LDAP/SCIM/directory provisioning, a generic custom-field/layout editor, visual arbitrary workflow automation, full DMARC/TLS-report ingestion and multi-instance hosting are later/outside this application release. Their present disposition is owner links or absence, not dead menu placeholders. Custom model/editor work must first establish its own security, migration and domain constraints. Existing framework agent triggers, service jobs, registered webhooks and domain automations already cover the supported automation paths. Browser database restore remains an advanced maintenance operation rather than an unguarded future button.

Two reference features are later (review §6):
- **Passkeys.** A WebAuthn factor type in the framework, beside TOTP, single-use, email and SMS, with Commons and UserDetail forms (Odoo `auth_passkey`).
- **Event-condition notification rules.** Send on a record condition or a date offset, over DataFeed RT push and NotificationTopic (Frappe Notification, Odoo `base_automation`).

## 19. Open

Installation-specific delegation bundles, provider credentials, organization memberships, mail-domain records, policy durations, backup destinations and enabled protocol ports are configuration inputs chosen through the guarded owner workflows above. The review's Open items on phasing, import strategy, agent screens, the invitation route, webhook scope, Tools screens for `ADMIN` and archive download are decided in §18 (review Z1, Z2, Z5).

1. **Per-group session lock and inactivity timeout** (review ADM-F02). *Recommendation:* add framework UserGroup flags beside `requireAuthcFactor`: an inactivity timeout and a re-authentication interval for members. The strictest of a user's groups applies. Edit them in SignInPolicy GroupSignInRules in A2, with `check#AdministrationPath` refusing a change that locks out every administrator. This is a framework need; until it exists, the `user-facade` session settings apply to everyone.
2. **Runtime-editable password policy** (review ADM-F04). *Recommendation:* keep `user-facade` `password` in conf for the first release. Later, a framework typed runtime setter over the same attributes, bounded below by the conf values so a runtime edit can only tighten them, audited and shown in EffectiveSignInPolicy. No database copy of conf.
3. **Saved export profiles** (review ADM-F06). *Recommendation:* later, after DataExport. A profile per export target stores fields and filters only, never grants. It is owned by its creator and shared through a group, and running it rechecks the caller's fields and rows.
4. **Connection and protocol checks on Monitor** (review R-ADM-L02). *Recommendation:* register the HTTP-shaped checks as Monitor synthetic checks, so they get schedules, history, traces and alerting with no Admin scheduler: gateway endpoints, identity-provider metadata URLs and remote `sendUrl`s. TCP, DNS and certificate-expiry checks stay `test#Protocol` until Monitor gains those check kinds; that is a framework ask for Remaining-Work (checks are HTTP only today).
5. **Privacy intake before PrivacyRequests ships.** *Recommendation:* the PASS Account privacy page ships with PrivacyRequests (later). Until then a data-subject request arrives as a Customer Service case of a privacy type, restricted to the privacy administrator. It is recorded as a PrivacyRequest, with the case as its source reference, when that screen ships.
6. **New-device sign-in window.** *Recommendation:* 90 days without a matching client in UserLoginHistory and Visit, as an instance setting. A shorter window is noisy for roaming users; a longer one keeps more history than login records need. The setting is built (impl-admin P8): the instance preference `signin.newDeviceWindowDays`, 90 by default, 1 to 730, which `notify#NewDeviceSignIn` reads (built in impl-admin P10; a client is the browser and system with an IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /48 network, §4 Sessions).
7. **What a wall display reads as** (CX-DEVICE). Settled (impl-admin P18): a display account, as §6 Devices describes: a UserAccount nothing signs in, with a Person party and a member relationship to each organization it shows and the registered bundles its registrar could assign, which is the user of a device's request only, within the device's route group. Checked against CX-DEVICE (a device identifies no person: the display account is none, and a kiosk's actions still identify theirs by badge and PIN) and against the framework's sign-in and session code (the account is never a session's user). design-insights §4 Playlists follows it.
