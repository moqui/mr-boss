IronStone Maintenance serves facilities and equipment managers, planners, technicians and resource approvers. It covers facilities, equipment and assets, corrective and preventive work, reusable procedures with execution checklists, and facility/equipment scheduling administration. Requesters report problems through shared intake; technicians execute internal work and linked customer repairs. A small organization can use Maintenance with the authorized shared screens without acquiring another app's launcher grant.

## 1. Shell

### 1.1 Root, authority and presentation

Root `screen/Maintenance.xml`, route `Maintenance`, keeps `sym_o_build`. `BOSS_MAINTENANCE` is the existing artifact-group name and the proposed ordinary root user group. Current setup also has `BOSS_MAINTENANCE_ADMIN` and framework ADMIN root authorizations; the root comment saying all access is commented out is stale.

The root continues `mantle.party.PartyServices.setup#UserOrganizationInfo`. Ordinary groups use `MANTLE_USER_ORG` and `MANTLE_PRIVATE_CONTACT`; the app administrator uses `MANTLE_ACTIVE_ORG` and the private-contact filter. Services independently check organization, facility, resource, work-order and action scope. An assignment, `appRoot`, supplied owner or another app's edit grant is not read authority. A customer-owned asset is reachable through an authorized repair/custody relationship, not by changing its owner to the service organization.

Proposed groups grant Moqui permissions in setup data: Mantle seeds the maintenance, process and resource permissions, since its services check them and it stands without BOSS, and BossSetupData grants them to the groups (impl-maintenance §4). Services use permissions, not `isInGroup` or caller-name tests. Root access opens navigation; it does not expose all work or financial values.

Stock actions also require the current Warehouse stock permission for the source/destination facility; buying and financial actions retain their owners' permissions. Equipment movement uses the stock movement guard where stock is affected. Read authority is always explicit: broad work view, assigned-work view and own-request view are separate permissions; a write permission alone opens no list. Costs, supplier prices and restricted employee rates are omitted from unauthorized service answers, exports, shell context and prints. The service organization on a work order is authoritative even when its target is customer-owned.

The deny rows (`BOSS_MNT_SETTINGS`, `BOSS_MNT_REQUESTS`, `BOSS_MNT_COSTS`) and the groups' ALWAYS rows, each carried by its bundle, stay beside the gates. The administrator holds every `MAINTENANCE_*`, `WORK_PROCESS_*` and `RESOURCE_*` permission, `MAINTENANCE_ALL_ORGANIZATIONS` among them and in that group's bundle alone, and not `FACILITY_EDIT`; the planner, supervisor and resource administrator also hold People's `PEOPLE_DIRECTORY_VIEW`, so Employee360's public work card opens for them (impl-maintenance F62). A shared device's request calls no context service and is served no Chatter or RecordFiles panel (F74); any other request is served the panels of Maintenance's own record hosts only, which their phases add, and a panel request naming no record: from P2 equipment (Asset) and facilities, each checked as `check#Record` checks it (impl-maintenance F82); from P3 a work order and its steps (WorkEffort, a step checked as its work order), and Mantle's `get#RecordLinkInfo` and `search#Universal` admit a work order and its steps in every app only as MaintenanceAccess does (`workHidden`) (a maintenance reader of its service organization, its technician, a party of the work; impl-maintenance F69), beside People's protected tasks; from P4 a maintenance request (Request), a planner's or supervisor's of its organization. As built (P4): a requester (`BOSS_MAINTENANCE_REQUEST`) holds no Maintenance root and no `ironstone.maintenance` grant (impl-maintenance F78): Commons' Report a problem (its own artifact group, `BOSS_MNT_REPORT_PROBLEM`) and RequestDetail call Mantle's intake services, which scope a requester to their own organizations (`MaintenanceAccess.requesterOrganizations`) and their own requests.

XML is the default, including list/board/calendar/tree views already in the XSD. Every screen works in `qvt` and `tui`; custom Commons calendars keep their existing terminal alternative. WorkOrderDetail, intake, meters and booking decisions work at phone width. Lists have record links, names, state words/chips, server paging, saved finds and two useful mobile columns. Record screens use the standard header, one current primary action, More actions and named confirmations. Errors preserve input; inaccessible records have a not-available state; missing measurements or costs are not zero.

## 4. Procedures and execution checklists

### 4.1 One reusable WorkEffort process

A process definition is a root WorkEffort with `isTemplate=Y` and ordered child WorkEfforts, also marked templates. Maintenance roots use `WetTask` and `WepMaintenance`; steps are tasks with their own ordered child groups. Execution roots and children are fresh WorkEfforts with template flags cleared. The existing template flag's project-oriented description needs generalizing; no parallel Procedure/ProcedureRun entity replaces these identities.

Proposed reusable WorkEffortProcessPolicy and WorkEffortStepPolicy rows carry effective-dated configuration that ordinary WorkEffort fields lack. Process policy holds publication/retirement effectivity and applicability; step policy holds required flag, response kind, measurement definition/bounds, evidence requirement, assignable role, due offset and waiver allowance. WorkEffort fields retain names, descriptions, effort and order. A process's publication records an approval decision and effective time; approved content is amended by a new effective change. Do not introduce a separate version/snapshot tree beside WorkEffort.

The two records are built in two parts (impl-maintenance §4, impl-people §4). People, built first, makes both under these names and minimal: WorkEffortProcessPolicy with the using policy and a guards-execution flag, false for its checklists, and WorkEffortStepPolicy with the role, due offset and required flag, with `store#ProcessStep` and the two-pass `instantiate#WorkProcess` of §4.2. Maintenance extends the records and the two services with publication, approval and effectivity, evidence, readings, waivers and the checked path of §4.2, and sets the guards-execution flag for its procedures; People's checklists must keep working unchanged. A maintenance template is therefore a `WetTask` of purpose `WepMaintenance` whose process policy guards execution, and every template list filters on all three, since Manufacturing's routing templates set `isTemplate` too (impl-maintenance F4).

As built (impl-people P7, the first part): `mantle.work.effort.WorkEffortProcessPolicy` (workEffortId and fromDate, thruDate, `processPolicyEnumId` of the new type WorkProcessPolicy, `guardsExecution` default N, an execution's `operationKey`) and `WorkEffortStepPolicy` (workEffortId and fromDate, thruDate, `isRequired`, `roleTypeId`, `dueOffsetDays`) in `entity/WorkProcessEntities.xml`; `mantle.work.ProcessServices` `store#ProcessStep`, `instantiate#WorkProcess` and `check#ManagedWork` over `WorkProcesses.js`, whose table `policies` names the permission each using policy's processes are changed and instantiated with (People's `WppPeopleChecklist`: `PEOPLE_HR_EDIT`), to which Maintenance adds its own. The managed-work check is called by the generic task and work-effort services and also reads a root's `mantle.work.maintenance.MaintenanceWorkOrder` once that entity is defined. Mantle's `test/work/WorkProcess` is the spec that must pass unchanged after Maintenance's P6.

Use WorkEffortAssetNeeded, WorkEffortSkillStandard and planned WorkEffortProduct rows for requirements; add only the step-policy fields they lack. `WorkEffortSkillStandard` has no level today; it gains proposed `minimumSkillLevel`, shared with People PE-14, and a step may name a required qualification type that assignment checks through People's `check#Qualifications` (review Y8). As built (P8): the qualification is the skill standard's own (`WorkEffortSkillStandard.qualificationTypeEnumId`, "proved by", as a job class requirement's), set with the least level by `store#ProcessRequirements` and copied to the work made of the procedure; People's `check#Qualifications` reads both for a work effort. A restricted kind (People's identity documents and work permits) proves no skill: `store#ProcessRequirements` refuses it, the choice leaves it out and People's staffing answer never asks it for dispatch (P8 review). Group nodes organize work and derive completion from children; actionable leaves carry responses. `sequenceNum` is display order, not an implicit blocking dependency. `WeatDependsOn` edges encode required predecessor completion within the same template; cycle and cross-template edges are refused. Independent leaves may run concurrently. No conditional branches, loops or arbitrary expressions in the core editor.

Template state is Draft (`WeInPlanning`), Approved (`WeApproved`) and Retired (`WeClosed`) under a template-specific status flow (`WorkEffortProcedure`). Publication effectivity is separate from execution status. Templates cannot accrue time, issue stock, reserve equipment, satisfy PM, send technician assignments, appear in a CalDAV Tasks collection or count in operational reports. Publication/retirement notices are allowed. Process authoring and execution permissions are separate; the approver is distinct from the last author by default, with any self-approval exception an explicit audited organization policy, off by default.

As built (impl-maintenance P6): the policy `WppMaintenanceProcedure` joins WorkProcesses.js' `policies` with its own edit permission (`WORK_PROCESS_EDIT`) and instantiation permissions (`MAINTENANCE_PLAN` or `_SUPERVISE`, through `create#MaintenanceWorkOrder`) and its checked path, `mantle/work/MaintenanceProcedures.js`, which `store#ProcessStep` and `instantiate#WorkProcess` run for its procedures; People's path and its spec are unchanged (F58). WorkEffortProcessPolicy gained `effectiveFromDate`, `effectiveThruDate`, `approvedByUserId`, `approvedDate`, `lastAuthorUserId`, `lastAuthoredDate`, the applicability (`maintenanceTypeEnumId`, `applicableProductId`, `applicableAssetClassEnumId`, `applicableFacilityTypeEnumId`), `sourceWorkEffortId` and `publishedContentId`; WorkEffortStepPolicy gained `responseKindEnumId` (WorkStepResponseKind: check off, pass or fail, reading), `measurementTypeId`, `measurementUomId`, `minimumValue`, `maximumValue`, `evidenceRequired`, `commentRequired`, `waiverAllowed` and `changeReason`, carried by `storeStep` (a change starts the next row) and copied by `instantiate`. A procedure is a template root of `WetTask` and `WepMaintenance` owned by its organization, its policy guarding execution, so the template, its steps and every execution are managed work (`check#ManagedWork`). An author's change of an approved procedure takes it back to Draft (amended) and cancels a request waiting; work made earlier keeps its rows; there is no versioned definition. A group is a step with steps under it and takes no response. The approval is a `WorkEffortApproval` row (requested, approved or refused, F44), sent to the organization's approvers on `WorkProcessApproval` (Mantle's topic and `TopicRegistration`; approve and refuse services `approve#` and `refuse#WorkProcess`), never by the last author unless the organization's `MaintenancePolicy.procedureSelfApproval` allows it; approval checks the definition (a group with a response, a reading without a measurement type or unit, a unit of another kind, bounds the wrong way round, a dependency outside the procedure or in a loop, no step: `PUBLICATION_INVALID`). A step's instruction is a draft (`WectProcedureDraft`) until approval writes it as a `WectInstruction` row in the shape of Manufacturing's rule (the file once, its sha256, the effective date, the one before ended; F68); approval also stores the definition as a PDF (`WectPublishedProcedure`, template/work/Procedure.print.html.fancy). The planned requirements are WorkEffortProduct (consumed), WorkEffortAssetNeeded and WorkEffortSkillStandard rows of the step (`store#ProcessRequirements`; no skill level until People's P18, F75). The estimate of a procedure's work is its steps' sum when a step has one: Mantle's WorkEffortUpdateProject rollup already writes that sum on the root.

### 4.2 Instantiate and preserve execution identity

Proposed `mantle.work.ProcessServices.instantiate#WorkProcess` is a checked, atomic two-pass operation in a module of its own: `clone#WorkEffort` and `clone#Project` are not changed, because a single-pass copy of every field shares almost nothing with it and Projects' callers stay as they are (impl-maintenance §4, F5). People's plan builds its first, minimal form (ID map, remapped dependencies, one `WeatBasedOn` link per node) and Maintenance adds the checks below. It is the common mechanism used by Maintenance's create/generate services. People's lifecycle checklists use it too (review Y5), with a People policy that has no evidence, readings or waivers and a lifecycle-event root of purpose `WepPrOrgMgmt`; People acquires no Maintenance policy. It does not convert manufacturing routings into this model or rewrite another app's templates.

1. Validate an approved template effective at the requested creation time, organization, applicability, tree shape, dependencies and actor authority. Lock/read a consistent definition and create the operation identity before mutation. Ordinary create uses now; historical imports use an explicitly authorized as-of path.
2. Clone only definition fields and the effective child tree. Build an old→new workEffortId map. Set the new service organization, parent/root IDs and maintenance flow; reset `isTemplate`, resolution, status, percent/actual/remaining effort, actual/estimated dates and recurrence fields. Derive new estimates/due dates from durations and offsets rather than retaining a previous job's dates.
3. Copy planned requirements, approved instruction content and step policies as execution-owned data; reset fulfillment/results. Do not copy assignments, time entries, checks, approvals, asset issuances/receipts, activity history, comments, customer links or source bookings. Roles are requirements until an authorized assignment resolves them.
4. Remap internal dependency edges to the cloned IDs, then add one `WeatBasedOn` association from each execution node to its definition node, with the source effective time. Expose both provenance and the current execution identity. The generic clone's `copyAssoc=true` is not sufficient because it retains source targets.
5. Commit the complete tree with the work-order extension, AssetMaintenance/link and PM occurrence where applicable. A failed child invalidates this one execution; separate selected templates/resources continue with named outcomes. Replaying the same operation key and request returns the same root; changed inputs under the key are refused.

An execution's own rows are business records with independent identity, not a forbidden parallel snapshot subsystem. Approved instruction resources referenced at execution creation must remain resolvable as of that time; mutable URLs alone do not preserve instructions. Use retained resource revisions or copied immutable instruction content and stored print output. Later template edits/retirement affect future creation only. An authorized amendment to an open execution records a reason and invalidates affected completion/approval checks; it does not silently refresh from the template.

As built (impl-maintenance P6): `create#MaintenanceWorkOrder` takes `templateWorkEffortId` (and `roleParties`, the organization's people for the steps' roles) and runs the checked path in its transaction: the caller's instantiation permission and scope, the procedure of the work's organization, Approved and in effect now (`PROCESS_NOT_APPROVED`, `PROCESS_RETIRED`, `PROCESS_NOT_EFFECTIVE`), applicable to the equipment's model or class or the facility's type (`PROCESS_NOT_APPLICABLE`); then People's two-pass instantiate (ID map, remapped `WeatDependsOn` edges, one `WeatBasedOn` link a node, the step and process policies), then each template node's requirements copied to its new node; the root then becomes the work order (`MaintenanceWorkOrder.procedureWorkEffortId` and `procedureDate`). Work from a procedure is never approved on create as corrective work is; the threshold reads its steps' estimates. Instructions are not copied: `find#ProcessInstructions` resolves an execution node's instruction through its `WeatBasedOn` link, the template node's `WectInstruction` in effect when the execution was made. The generic `clone#WorkEffort`, `add#TaskTime`, `add#` and `remove#TaskDependency` refuse managed work, and Projects' time import names a managed item not available (F110, F111).

### 4.3 Responses, evidence and completion

Core response kinds are Check off, Pass/fail and Reading. Check off records done; Pass/fail records the observed result; Reading records a decimal value, measurement type/UOM and applicable bounds. Each can require a comment and/or attached evidence. Reuse `mantle.work.measurement.Measurement` for step readings; AssetMeter remains the PM counter store (§5). A counter reading captured in a step uses the one meter-writing action and links the Measurement evidence, rather than writing two unrelated readings.

Proposed WorkEffortCheck is an execution fact with its own ID: step, response kind, observed outcome, measurement reference if applicable, observed date, actor/time, notes and superseded-check reference. A small evidence join names the accepted WorkEffortContent IDs. Rechecks append facts; the current accepted check is explicit and previous failures remain visible. A waiver is a separate reasoned decision associated with that step/check and approver, not a rewritten Pass. Server validation resolves required inputs from the execution's step policy.

StepResponse accepts workEffortId, response kind/outcome, observedDate, measurement type/value/UOM when applicable, notes, evidence content IDs, optional supersededCheckId and correction reason, expected update time and operationKey. It never trusts supplied completion actor, policy, minimum/maximum or root organization. Completion rejects unmet dependencies, missing evidence, wrong units, out-of-range values and unauthorized assignments. A failed observation can be saved while leaving the step unresolved. A supervisor can waive only a requirement whose execution policy allows waiver; the observed failure remains. Optional steps may be skipped with a reason; required steps need completion or a valid waiver. Structural groups have no independent response.

Starting an eligible leaf uses WeInProgress; an accepted response or allowed waiver makes it WeComplete, with the observed/waived outcome separate from status. A skipped optional leaf is WeCancelled with a recorded skip disposition. Groups derive readiness from their children. Root Close moves completed children/groups to WeClosed and retains cancelled children and all check history. These transitions pass through the process guard even when invoked through shared WorkItemDetail; approving the root authorizes its current execution plan without individually publishing the child tasks as templates.

Accepted evidence cannot be edited, unlinked or deleted to make a completed check misleading. Corrections append replacement evidence/results and invalidate affected acceptance, with audit and permission checks through generic record-file and task APIs too. Closed work requires supervisor correction; the original facts stay available. Core files are ordinary evidence, not a regulated digital signature claim.

As built (impl-maintenance P7): `mantle/work/ProcessChecks.js` over ProcessServices' `get#ProcessExecution`, `record#ProcessStep`, `waive#ProcessStep`, `amend#ProcessExecution`, `transcribe#CheckVoiceNote`, `get#ProcessChecklist`, `get#EvidencePolicy` and `check#EvidenceChange`. The records (entity/WorkProcessEntities.xml): `WorkEffortCheck` (the step, response kind, outcome Done, Pass, Fail or Skipped, the Measurement and the value and unit as entered, observed and recorded time, who, notes, a voice note's transcript, the findings, a superseded check and correction reason, its state Accepted, Unresolved, Superseded or Invalidated, an operation key), `WorkEffortCheckEvidence` (a check's files, a file or a voice note), `WorkEffortWaiver` (the supervisor's reasoned decision, naming the failed check, ended by an amendment that invalidates it) and `WorkEffortExecutionAmendment` (reason, what changed, what it invalidated). `record#ProcessStep` takes an action: start (a leaf in progress), skip (an optional leaf, cancelled with the reason in its check) or respond; a step's response, its group's derived state, its dependencies (its own and its groups', `DEPENDENCY_OPEN`) and the caller (`MAINTENANCE_EXECUTE` assigned to the step or the root, or a supervisor) are checked on the server, and the root must be in progress. A missing file, a missing comment, a reading in another unit, an out-of-range reading and a failure are saved as an unresolved check with its findings, leaving the step open; a recheck appends; a correction names the check it supersedes; an accepted check completes the step. Every reading is a Measurement of the step with the work's equipment; a reading whose measurement type names a meter type also goes through `record#MaintenanceMeter` (only on the work's own equipment, F94), with the Measurement's meter linked. Evidence is uploaded with the response onto the work order's root (WectCheckEvidence, WectVoiceNote, named for the step), or named among the work order's files. A group takes no response (`GROUP_NO_RESPONSE`) and is complete when its children are done. The work order's Complete is refused while a required leaf is neither complete nor waived (`STEPS_OPEN`, and WorkOrderDetail's blocker list); Close moves complete nodes to Closed, cancels open optional ones and stores the completed checklist (WectCompletedChecklist, template/work/Checklist.print.html.fancy; a PDF that cannot be made is logged and the close stands); Cancel cancels the open nodes. A waiver is `MAINTENANCE_SUPERVISE`'s where the step's policy allows it (`WAIVER_NOT_ALLOWED`), with a reason; the failed checks stay. An amendment (supervisor, open work only, `WORK_CLOSED` otherwise) changes a step's policy fields from now or what it waits for; its preview names the acceptances it invalidates (a complete step whose requirement changed or that now waits for work not done) and the complete steps that wait for a reopened one; applying it invalidates the checks, ends the waivers and puts the steps back in progress. A check's evidence (and the stored checklist) is protected: RecordFiles' `get#ContentType` takes Mantle's evidence policy for a managed work effort (no removal, no new version, and no new file on closed or cancelled work) and `update#WorkEffortContent` refuses it (`EVIDENCE_PROTECTED`). A voice note is an uploaded audio file (no capture element, F38), transcribed by `SpeechServices.transcribe#Audio` in a transaction of its own after the response, its transcript kept on the check.

As built (P7 review, Nov 12): an amendment's change may also add a step (under the work order or a group; a complete group it joins reopens, named among the invalidations) or remove a step, or a group with its steps, that has not started and has no check (`STEP_STARTED` otherwise); a removed step is cancelled and named in the amendment, the steps that waited for it no longer do, and the gate and the checklist leave it out ("Removed"). A voice note is sent to the speech model only when the organization's `MaintenancePolicy.voiceNoteTranscription` is on (off by default; `TRANSCRIPTION_OFF`); the audio is kept either way (the user's calls of Nov 12). The evidence guard is the entity's (`WorkEffortEvidenceProtected` in Mantle's Work.eecas.xml): a check's evidence and a stored checklist are not updated or deleted through any path (the content services, RecordFiles, entity-auto, the entity REST), a check's link to its evidence is not changed, closed or cancelled managed work takes no new file and a completed checklist is stored only by the close; `save#WorkEffortContentFile` refuses new bytes for them. A waiver waits for the step's dependencies (`DEPENDENCY_OPEN`), as a response does.

## 5. Preventive maintenance

### 5.1 Schedule and due semantics

ProductMaintenance stays the equipment-model default. Proposed MaintenanceSchedule identifies one asset or facility and optional productMaintenanceId; effective-dated terms carry enabled dates, procedure, calendar interval or meter increment, anchor, lead window, assigned team and scheduling options. Facility schedules are calendar-based and need no ProductMaintenance or dummy asset. Equipment-specific terms explicitly override product defaults; the plan shows the origin of each value. Changing a product default does not mutate existing executions or already generated occurrences.

Core uses one trigger per schedule: calendar or meter. Several schedules can cover an asset. Calendar schedules default to fixed cadence from an explicit first due date in the resource's configured time zone; completion-relative cadence is an explicit choice. Month/year arithmetic preserves the intended day with last-day clamping. The due date remains the obligation date when work is moved to an allowed weekday or next business day; the move uses `mantle.work.EventServices.get#NextBusinessDay` over the WepHoliday categories of the schedule's holiday calendar, the same People HolidayCalendars that leave uses. Assigned PM work gets due reminders through `EventServices.store#WorkEffortReminders`. Lead time determines when to generate, not whether work is overdue. An unknown time zone blocks activation rather than assuming the server zone.

Meter schedules require an increasing cumulative meter, positive increment, UOM and initial baseline. Subsequent thresholds advance from accepted completion readings, never from an unfinished AssetMaintenance or merely the highest value. Select valid observations by reading time and correction provenance. Decreasing values are refused except an explicit audited reset/replacement with a new baseline; do not infer wraparound. Staleness is a configurable maximum age per schedule. Missing/stale readings show Unknown/stale and can request a reading; they never mean zero usage or Not due. Noncumulative measurements such as temperature are checklist readings, not cumulative PM triggers.

Retain AssetMeter as the canonical equipment-counter history for the first release. A reading taken during a step sets the existing `AssetMeter.workEffortId` to the step, and a reset or correction sets `readingReasonEnumId`, so no new link or reason field is needed. Its `(assetId, readingDate)` key means concurrent meter readings on one asset must have distinct supported timestamps; a write detects collisions and asks for a distinct observation time rather than overwriting another meter. A later Measurement migration requires an explicit compatibility/backfill design and is not bundled here. Correct the existing due helper only through a named opt-in maintenance policy/contract; the new due projection does not claim the legacy numeric helper already supports calendar intervals.

Proposed MaintenanceOccurrence has identity because a due obligation exists independently of its execution. It records schedule, due date/threshold, generation operation, linked work and disposition. Unique schedule/occurrence keys serialize manual/job retries. At most one unresolved generated execution per schedule in core. Missed calendar obligations stay visible; a planner explicitly coalesces missed dates into one catch-up execution with the covered occurrences recorded, or skips with reason. Re-running a job never silently marks missed PM compliant. Meter catch-up creates one due execution and advances only after a valid completion. Cancellation/skip does not count as completion or consume a completed repeat count; `ProductMaintenance.repeatCount` null/zero remains unlimited.

As built (impl-maintenance P13): a plan is `mantle.work.maintenance.MaintenanceSchedule` (one asset or one facility, an optional ProductMaintenance of the asset's model, its organization, Draft, Active or Ended, a revision and an operation key) with dated `MaintenanceScheduleTerm` rows (trigger, cadence, interval and unit or meter and increment, the first due as a local date and time in the target's zone (`firstDueLocal`, not an instant, so its wall time holds across a daylight-saving change), meter baseline and maximum reading age (30 days unless set), business days with a People holiday calendar, lead days, procedure, maintenance type, team, the change's reason). A term field left empty takes the model default's value and the plan names the origin of each. A draft's terms are edited in place until activation; from activation they are dated rows: a change starts from an effective time with a reason, the earlier term ended and kept, and a term already planned for later is never cut short (the user's call, Nov 12, as built). Activation needs the target's time zone; a procedure is optional, and one named, by the plan or its model default, must qualify (approved, in effect, applicable, the organization's; `PROCEDURE_NOT_QUALIFIED`); with none, P14 generates an ad hoc work order with the plan's name and estimate (`estimatedWorkTime`, hours) (the user's call, Nov 12, replacing the built `PROCEDURE_REQUIRED` refusal). A procedure stored on a plan is a maintenance procedure of its organization. A plan keeps its organization: once its target has moved to another, it is not changed or activated (`TARGET_MOVED`), only ended. A completion counts when it is a work order of the plan (`MaintenanceWorkOrder.maintenanceScheduleId`, which P14's generation sets and no other writer accepts) complete or closed, or a completed AssetMaintenance of the plan's model default; a fixed cadence's completion covers the earliest obligation not yet covered that falls before the next obligation after it, once it is later than the obligation one interval before the first (an older record covers nothing). A meter's baseline is the later of the plan's baseline and the reading the last counted completion took. A meter's observations leave out a reading `create#AssetMaintenance` made from a maintenance record's planned interval while that record is not completed; usage is summed across a reset. Holidays are the organization's WepHoliday events and the term's People holiday calendar (a WcatHoliday category), read by `EventServices.get#NextBusinessDay`, which People's HolidayCalendars write (impl-maintenance F64); the move needs the term's business-days flag; a weekday set other than Monday to Friday is not built.

Generation calls §4 when a procedure is configured; otherwise it creates an ad hoc root with instructions (the plan's name and estimate, the user's call of Nov 12).

As built (impl-maintenance P14): an obligation acted on is a `MaintenanceOccurrence` (`mantle.work.maintenance`), unique by plan and obligation key (a calendar obligation's local due date and time in the target's zone; a meter's `meter` and threshold) by a unique index, with its due date or threshold and the term it fell under: the projection walks each term's obligations from its start (the first term's from its first due date) to the next term, so a change of interval leaves earlier obligations where they fell (impl-maintenance F186). Its states are open, work made, completed, skipped and coalesced (into a catch-up, `coveredByOccurrenceId`). `generate#MaintenanceWorkOrders` (a planner's or supervisor's) and the `generate_MaintenanceWorkOrders` job (`run#MaintenanceGeneration`, seeded paused; it runs with no user, as a planner of every organization who is no supervisor, in batches of 100 plans by default, at most 5000 a run) take each plan in a transaction of its own with the plan locked: at most one unresolved execution a plan (`WORK_OPEN`), the next uncovered obligation once due (its lead days before the planned day; a meter at its threshold), blocked with a named reason for a stale or missing reading ("Request a reading"), a procedure that no longer qualifies or a target moved, and the work order made through the checked instantiation or ad hoc, preventive, from the schedule, the plan set on it by that path alone, so it waits for a supervisor unless within the threshold; one plan's failure is named and the rest go on, and an operation key answers a retry. A work order's completion or close credits its obligation once; a cancellation opens it again (a catch-up's coalesced obligations with it), and a reopened order credits nothing twice. `reconcile#MaintenanceSchedule` coalesces two or more missed due dates into one catch-up (its work made from now, or the open one), skips a due date, or corrects a counted completion (the obligation open again, its work order kept and named not counted, open work named for review), each with a reason; a meter plan's obligation is neither coalesced nor skipped. A meter plan's work order completes only with the reading of the plan's meter taken in it, recorded with Complete when it has none (`READING_REQUIRED`, impl-maintenance F187). Due reminders for assigned PM work (as built, P14 review): `schedule#MaintenanceWorkOrder` writes a reminder for each person it assigns to a plan's work, a day before its due date (the root's planned finish; `WorkEffortReminder`, sent by the reminder job on the `WorkEffortReminder` topic), and removes those of a person it takes off; generated work assigns the plan's team, no person, so the reminder comes with the assignment. The job's scope (every organization) is the job's alone: the scheduler runs it with no user, Admin's run outside the schedule marks it due for the scheduler, and a person who runs it otherwise generates as themselves. A meter plan's work completed by a supervisor records its reading as the completer, as the assigned technician does. Creating planned AssetMaintenance must not pass the future threshold as `intervalQuantity` to `create#AssetMaintenance`, because that service would manufacture an actual meter reading. Capture actual readings at execution through the validated meter writer. The legacy AsMnt completion date/interval are synchronized on actual completion, not generation. Correction of counted completion/reading recalculates future due projection and flags already generated work for review; it does not silently delete work or regress earlier obligations.

## 7. Scheduling administration

### 7.1 Shared booking and availability contract

Commons owns booking/event UI. Maintenance owns administration of the same resources, booking requests and blackout policy. Keep WorkEffortFacility statuses `WefRequested`, `WefAssigned`, `WefRequestDenied` and asset assignments `WeaaRequested`, `WeaaAssigned`, `WeaaRequestDenied`; do not add a parallel booking table. An approval identifies the event, resource and the particular assignment/occurrence span, not just a notification ID.

Proposed effective-dated ResourceBookingPolicy targets one asset or facility. It contains enabled/bookable state, approval mode (automatic or assigned approvers), booking horizon and maximum duration, allowed hours/calendar, conflict policy and override authority. Capacity/features support discovery, not fractional concurrent allocation of a room: core books one whole resource. Meeting attendees must fit people capacity when known. Existing resources with no explicit policy retain the current BookingApprover-based approval fallback. New managed policies default to no conflicts; required approval without an eligible approver blocks with a setup finding, never auto-approves. Approver assignments remain dated FacilityParty/AssetPartyAssignment BookingApprover roles.

`book#Resource`, `check#ResourceBookings`, `update#EventOccurrence`, free/busy, approval actions and the CalDAV event PUT must share one resource-policy/availability read. A phone that moves an event holding a resource booking passes the booking check first (review Y1). The CalDAV event PUT already checks `check#ResourceBookings` (Y1, built); it moves to the managed-resource policy read with the other callers. Serialize writes on the resource and affected interval, rechecking requested/assigned bookings, maintenance blocks and current policy before commit. Half-open intervals permit adjacent bookings. Declined, cancelled and ended assignments do not block. Resource reads must include conflicting bookings the actor cannot inspect, returning a busy interval and safe reason rather than another person's title. The whole requested recurrence range is checked within a bounded configured horizon; report unsupported range explicitly, never silently check just the first occurrence. Reject overlap within a recurring series too; the current same-series exclusion is not a sufficient general policy.

Approval is bound to the requested resource/time/policy facts. A material change rechecks and may return Assigned to Requested; configured approvers use the same guarded decision from Maintenance or Commons. Policy changes apply to new/changed requests; already approved bookings remain recorded and are flagged for review if a new blackout conflicts. An authorized soft-conflict override requires its explicit permission and recorded reason. A bare `allowConflicts=true` is not authorization. Actual outages, safety blocks, closed resources and maintenance hard blocks cannot be overridden by booking policy.

As built (impl-maintenance P15, `mantle/work/ResourcePolicy.js`): every booking path calls one guard with the resource row locked — `book#Resource`, `check#ResourceBookings` (and through it `update#EventOccurrence`, `store#EventRecurrence` for an event booking a managed resource, and the CalDAV event PUT, whose check-only call gets a refusal as a hard conflict and which checks a managed resource's series again after a rule changed with no new time), and `create#Event`'s equipment (a managed asset through `book#Resource`, its rule stored first). A facility's blackout locks the bookable facilities inside it, so a room's booking and its building's blackout are decided one after the other; Manufacturing's offline time locks its WorkCenter row, which its operation moves (transactions of their own after it) never write, and a booking of a facility locks the WorkCenter rows of it and the facilities above it before its own row (P16, F171), so a booking cannot land under a new offline block unseen and nothing waits in a cycle. A move (`check#ResourceBookings`) locks the resource before it reads the other bookings, as `book#Resource` and the decisions do (P16 review). Who reads requests (the user's call, Nov 12): a booking approver their resources' and decides them; a resource policy reader or administrator and a supervisor the scope's, read only (purpose masked when not shared, the requester's notes shown). An outage or planned block in force over the booking (an asset's own; a facility's own or one of the facilities above it) refuses it for every resource, allowConflicts or not, naming only "out of service" or the block's reason. A resource with a ResourceBookingPolicy in force is managed: not bookable, longer than its maximum, starting past its horizon, a recurring booking whose range from now runs past the horizon (365 days when the policy sets none; its past meetings do not count) or whose instances overlap, and approval by approvers with none named are refused; a conflict is booked over only where the policy allows an override, by a holder of RESOURCE_BOOKING_OVERRIDE who works for the resource's organization, with a reason (kept in the booking's comments); approval follows the policy's mode. A resource with no policy keeps the approver fallback; a recurring booking of it is checked against outages and blocks over its whole range up to 365 days, its conflicts with other bookings on the first instance as before (P16, F165). An inventory or supplies asset is booked as before P15: no policy or block applies (P16, F172). A conflict with an event the user may not see is named Busy with no id, for every resource. `get#ResourceAvailability` is the one read of a resource's intervals (assigned, requested, planned, actual) with safe labels; `get#FreeBusy` adds a resource's blocks (status down or blocked) and `get#FullCalendarData` answers them apart from its events (`unavailableList`), and answers a managed resource's booking the user may not open as Busy, with no title, link, place or organizer (an unmanaged resource's events are as before). Manufacturing's planning reads a blackout of a work center's plant or building as offline time. A capacity or feature is shown, never booked fractionally.

As built for an event an application keeps (impl-people follow-up 3, the user's call of Nov 12; F356): a People training session's or interview's room chosen on People's screens (Room) is a booking of these services, made and let go by People's own services as the system after their own check (Mantle `humanres/EventRooms.js`), so a development editor or recruiter who is not the organizer books it too, and the room's policy, other bookings, outages and blackouts apply to it and its approvers decide it; a move and a cancel go through `keptEventMoved` and `keptEventCancelled` as for any kept event; as the system a clash is named Busy (F358).

Proposed ResourceUnavailability records planned blocks and actual outages with separate kinds, source work/step or production-downtime link, resource, interval, reason and correction history. One source occurrence yields one block. Maintenance execution updates the linked block. One equipment outage fact (review Y3): Manufacturing's proposed `store#ProductionDowntime` with an equipment-fault reason creates or links one ResourceUnavailability (kind actual) in the same transaction, keeping its production reason and OEE interval; Maintenance owns restoration through `end#ResourceUnavailability`. ProductionDowntime may retain non-equipment reasons; it never creates a second equipment outage interval. Manufacturing resource planning and Commons booking consume the same availability projection. Asset.statusId retains inventory/lifecycle meaning.

An actual outage can be open-ended; a planned block requires an end. Recording a real fault is permitted even if it conflicts with existing reservations: retain those reservations, alert their owners safely and require explicit resolution. Restoration ends the outage only after required work/authorized disposition; elapsed planned end does not restore an actual outage. Facility blocks include descendants for availability queries; an equipment block affects only that asset unless an explicit work-center/facility relationship requires propagation. Resource movement checks both old/new facility effects. No automatic cancellation of customer appointments or production runs.

New guards are permission/data checks in Mantle, not caller-stack tests or self-guarding EECAs. Compatibility changes to existing broad behavior use owner policy flags, off by default for unmanaged records, per crosscut. Flags do not disable security for records explicitly managed by the new workflow: the `MANAGED_WORK` refusal is behind no setting, since the MaintenanceWorkOrder row and the set guards-execution flag exist only where Maintenance made them (impl-maintenance §4). Notifications run after successful commit and use source identity to avoid duplicates.
