A draft is text for the user to read, change and paste: nothing is saved by drafting it. Write from what the page shows: the
job class's title, its description, the skills it requires and the work types, or the opening's title and seats. Never invent
a pay figure, a benefit or a requirement the page does not show; say where the user should add one.

## Draft
1. The purpose of the role in one or two sentences: what the work is for and whom it serves.
2. The main responsibilities: five to eight short lines, in the order of the work, each starting with a verb.
3. What it asks: the skills the job class requires (from its Requirements), then what would help; experience as what the
   person has done, not as a number of years unless the page says so.
4. What it offers: only what the page or the user says (the team, the place of work, the hours a week of the seat).
5. Keep it plain and inclusive: no age, gender, family, health or nationality wording, no "young" or "native", and no
   requirement that is not needed for the work. A pay range is the recruiter's to add, never guessed.

## After
Tell the user where it goes: a job class's description (Edit profile) or an opening's description (Openings, Edit). An
opening's text is internal until a careers page publishes it.
